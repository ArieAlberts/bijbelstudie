# Voice and editing guideline (stemtoets)

Applies to every text published under Arie Alberts' name in this repository: the
`body_nl` lezing, `study_body_nl`, `worksheet_body_nl`, articles in
`content/artikelen/`, and any download built from them. The English fields
follow the same rules in translation.

Status: October 2026. This guideline replaces older style rules where they
differ (short "hammer" sentences, paradox as opening line, stress accents such
as "máár", wij-form everywhere, a fixed closing formula). It is aligned with
Arie's *Volledig stemprofiel* (version 2, chapter 20) and his
*Artikelen & Facebook schrijfrichtlijn* (v3).

## Why

In October 2026 thirteen of Arie's spoken texts (transcriptions, ~15,300
words) were compared with fifteen published lezingen (~46,300 words). The
theology matched; the finish did not. Editing had added a recognisable
pattern that readers and detectors flag as AI-written:

| Signal (per 1000 words) | Spoken | Published |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Em dashes (—) | 0.7 | 14.9 |
| Stress accents (máár, Híj, zélf) | almost none | 16.9 |
| "ik denk" | 0.85 | 0.02 |
| Sentences of ≤ 5 words | 6% | 20% |
| grijpen / gegrepen | 0.33 | 1.22 |

## Starting point

> Mijn teksten moeten niet alleen weergeven wat ik geloof of concludeer, maar
> ook hoe ik daartoe kom. Mijn vragen, ervaringen, voorbehouden en ontdekkingen
> zijn onderdeel van mijn boodschap. Redactie mag die begrijpelijker maken,
> maar niet vervangen door een kunstmatig ritme of een vooraf vaststaande
> conclusie.

Voice and conviction are not the same thing. A text can match Arie's theology
completely and still not sound like him. Every text therefore gets two
separate checks: a content check (Scripture and the Frame method) and a voice
check (against the transcription, when there is one).

## How Arie reasons

He usually starts from a real occasion (a memory, an experience from work or
family, something he noticed in the text) and moves:

**waarnemen → onderzoeken → vergelijken → nuanceren → persoonlijke betekenis → uitnodigen**

The order varies. Keep the route visible; a text that keeps only the
conclusion is clearer but less recognisably his.

## Editing rules

1. Start at the real occasion, not at a catchy thesis.
2. Keep the thinking visible: "ik moest denken aan", "wat mij opviel",
   "ik denk dat". Keep the reservations he actually voiced; never add new
   ones. The point is the right degree of certainty, not particular words.
3. Remove speech noise ("eh", false starts, plain repetition). Keep
   meaningful hesitation and self-correction.
4. Keep concrete details, names, places and numbers. Keep an anecdote at the
   length he told it.
5. Keep his acknowledgement of the other side ("ik heb er niets tegen, als
   het goed wordt toegepast").
6. A paradox is the resolution of a story or thought, not the opening line,
   and is nuanced straight away.
7. Follow the grijpen → ontvangen movement where the passage carries it, also
   if that is in several places; never as a fixed heading or summary.
8. Write flowing sentences with calm connecting sentences. No stress accents
   for emphasis, few em dashes, no runs of short slogan sentences, no
   rhetorical questions to the audience ("Ziet u het?").
9. Use "niet … maar …" only where the contrast is really carried.
10. A few meaningful section headings, not one per thought.
11. Plain words ("kwetsbaar" where "fragiel" adds nothing).
12. Person as he speaks: "ik" where he testifies, "wij" for shared
    application, "je" when addressing the reader. Never "u" in lezingen.
13. The closing fits the genre: a personal blessing or a return to the
    opening image (overdenking), a real open question (practical or critical
    piece), or the discovered insight itself. No fixed formula.
14. Make source layers visible: what the text says, which link between
    passages is being made, what is personal symbolism, what is application.
    Exegesis added later is never presented as if he said it.
15. Names: he alternates Jezus, de Heere Jezus and Yeshua when speaking.
    Yeshua is the written default; do not force a single name everywhere.

## Voice check (against the transcription)

- Is the occasion really his?
- Can you point out which sentences go back to the recording and which were
  added later?
- Did the strongest personal details, names and numbers survive?
- Did his own reservations, nuances and acknowledgement of the other side
  survive, and was no certainty added without evidence?
- Does the emotion come from the experience or from the styling?
- Is every "niet … maar" needed?
- Would he say this out loud in a conversation, without it sounding like an
  acquired preaching voice?

## Content check (Scripture and Frame)

- Is the central Scripture link verifiable and correctly referenced?
- Are source layers distinguished (see rule 14)?
- Did an edit increase certainty without extra evidence?
- Are factual claims (regulation, history, dates) correct?

## Measuring stick, not a target

Quick counts show whether the finish is drifting. Watch them; do not write
towards them.

- Em dashes: spoken ≈ 0.7 per 1000 words; look again above ≈ 3.
- Stress accents: look again as soon as a pattern appears.
- Reservations from the recording: look again if they disappeared.

Sentence length is not a reliable measure, because speech recognition decides
where a spoken sentence ends.

## Workflow: two tracks

1. Arie records the text; it is transcribed.
2. Track A: a minimally corrected spoken version.
3. Track B: a publication version with the same order of argument.
4. Log each intervention: cut, reorder, sharpen, source check, or added content.
5. Arie reads both aloud and chooses.
