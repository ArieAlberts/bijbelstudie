A practical aid

Read the Parashah Yourself

The text is a land. Every interpretation is a map. A good map helps us see the land, but it must never invent the land. And this land is special: here God speaks to us.

Read the method Go to the parashah

This booklet wants to teach us something that sounds simple but takes a lifetime of practice: to listen before we interpret. A parashah is not a pantry from which we quickly take a beautiful thought. It is a portion of God’s Word into which we may enter, where we may stand and allow ourselves to be addressed.

The core is simple: we first listen to what the text actually gives and then keep our interpretation open to correction.

“Speak, for Your servant is listening.” — 1 Samuel 3:10

This site turns Arie Alberts’s handbook into a practical guide: a place to read the parashah for yourself. The guided reading keeps us close to the text; the method offers explanation when it is needed, with a fully worked example and the appendices for reference.

The method

Map and land

Whenever we read a text from Scripture, we look through a lens: our questions, our upbringing and what we have heard before. That is not wrong — without a lens we often see nothing. It becomes dangerous when we forget that we are wearing a lens and quietly equate our way of seeing with God’s own speech. The Frame therefore continually distinguishes between two things: receiving (the hand remains open and correctable) and grasping (the text must confirm our interpretation).

Five layers, in the right order

Just as we cut and weigh ingredients before mixing them when we cook, so in reading we keep five moments of attention distinct — not as separate rooms, but as a sequence.

1. What does the text say?
Words, actions, repetitions, contrasts and structure.
It goes wrong when: we already write down our interpretation as if it were an observation.
2. How is it experienced?
Desire, fear, hope, shame, wonder and imagery.
It goes wrong when: we immediately turn our experience into evidence.
3. What is revealed about God and humanity?
Source, calling, covenant, sin, grace and destination.
It goes wrong when: we go further than the ordinary meaning can bear.
4. What do we confess?
Its coherence with the whole of Scripture and with Yeshua.
It goes wrong when: we present our confession of faith as a neutral observation.
5. What does it ask of us?
Response, obedience, rhythm, community and daily practice.
It goes wrong when: the application becomes larger than the Word that carries it.

Five questions for every text

Open hands with which we receive what a passage actually gives — not a trick that always produces the same answer.

1
What is given, said or done here?
2
How do the people in the text itself view what is happening?
3
Where do the authority, strength or gift come from?
4
What movement do we see: from where to where?
5
Is something received and answered, or grasped and controlled?

Wait to interpret until you have looked carefully

Waiting is not passive. Mary treasured words in her heart. First we must be able to say of each section: this is what it says; this is a possible interpretation; this remains open. Only then do we apply the four questions.

Four questions for weighing

Above all four stands one foundational question: are we confusing our interpretation with what the text itself says?

Who is speaking, and where does it come from?
Does the direction come from God, or from fear, power, habit, rivalry or reaction? Is strength received as a gift, or is the means turned into the source?
Where does this come from and where is it going?
What earlier event continues to work here? Is there a path, a reversal or a fulfilment? Or does the interpretation merely circle around its own correctness?
What is given, and what is asked?
Does the gift come first and is obedience a response? Or must a person perform first in order to receive?
Is it truly coherent, or merely self-consistent?
Does the interpretation fit the words, the context, the whole of Scripture and life? Can the text still contradict it?

Two labels: how certain is it, and what may it do?

The first label tells us how certain something is. The second tells us what we may do with it. Both are necessary — an established fact is not automatically evidence for a spiritual conclusion.

How certain?
Confession
We believe this within the coherence of Scripture and faith in Yeshua.
Established
Clearly verifiable from words, grammar, history or reliable research.
Possible
It may help as an image or a deeper perspective, but it cannot carry a necessary conclusion.
Uncertain / wrong
There is too little support, or the interpretation goes beyond what the text can bear.
What may it do?
Carry
A main conclusion or central movement can genuinely rest on this.
Support
It confirms something already carried by stronger evidence.
Illustrate
It makes something visible or tangible, but proves nothing.
Do not use
Too weak, too speculative or methodologically mistaken.

The seven stages and the feasts

The feasts of Israel order the movement of a study: from rest, through looking and separating, to interpreting, awakening, reconciliation, dwelling and testing in rest. The feasts do not create the meaning — the text determines what is said.

Do not only dismantle — build something habitable

The hinge of every study: we do not merely expose what is distorted; we also put in its place an alternative carried by the text.

“Do not trust in your own strength.“
Receive even your strength as a gift and use it gratefully under God’s authority.
“Do not be stiff-necked.“
Let your heart be opened so that love for God and for the vulnerable becomes visible.
“Do not grasp your calling.“
Receive your name, lay aside what covers it and learn to carry it faithfully.
“Do not turn obedience into merit.“
Truly respond to grace and let obedience become fruit.

Do you receive it, or seize it?

The sharpest question in The Frame: do we hold truth, calling and grace as a received gift, or do we try to secure them with our own hands?

The gift comes first; we respond to it.
We secure the outcome through technique, pressure or a trick.
Our interpretation remains correctable by the text.
The text must prove our interpretation right.
Our interpretation can be corrected.
Every counterexample becomes fresh confirmation.
The human being remains a person and a responsible agent.
The human being becomes a tool, a mass, a culprit or a piece of evidence.
We remain in relationship with the Giver.
The Giver becomes a means of retaining the gift.
“This word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, so that you may do it.” — Deuteronomy 30:14

Common mistakes

The message was already fixed before we looked.
First write a bare list of what is actually there.
An application is presented as “the meaning.”
Say, “this may teach us,” and name the boundary.
A word root, number or letter image determines the sentence.
Return to grammar, usage and context.
Every detail is made into a reference to Yeshua.
Give the connection a label; preserve the text’s own character.
We only dismantle.
Put a text-supported, habitable alternative in its place.
Receiving becomes doing nothing.
Preserve the movement: given → answered → carried.
Every objection proves us right.
State what could genuinely disprove the interpretation.
The contrast becomes too absolute.
Ask whether motives can be mixed and allow nuance.
The application becomes a new checklist.
End with God’s initiative, grace and one walkable step.

Example

Ekev: the bread we did not know

Torah: Deuteronomy 8–10 · Haftarah: Isaiah 49 · Gospel reading: Matthew 4

What we see in the Torah

  • Israel is reminded of the entire journey through the wilderness.
  • Hunger and manna are named together: lack and received bread.
  • The warning not to forget God sounds precisely amid food, houses, flocks and possessions.
  • “My strength” is corrected by: He is the One who gives strength.
  • The land is emphatically not given because of Israel’s own righteousness.
“Remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you strength to produce wealth.” — Deuteronomy 8:18

The four questions, briefly

Where does it come from?
Bread, land and strength are given. Human strength is a means and a response, not the source.
Where is it moving?
From wilderness to land, from hunger to abundance — and precisely in abundance the danger of forgetting grows.
What is given and what is asked?
God gives and reminds; Israel is genuinely called to love, listen and walk faithfully.
Is it truly coherent?
Deuteronomy 9 prevents grace from being separated from responsibility; Isaiah 49 prevents human unfaithfulness from being turned into forgetfulness on God’s part.

The distorted frame and the habitable alternative

“My strength has produced this.“
Even my strength is received; I use it gratefully and remember the Giver.
“Because it is grace, obedience does not matter.“
Grace is the soil from which a genuine response grows.
“My emptiness proves that God has forgotten me.“
My feeling is heard, but God’s faithfulness has the final word.
“I must prove who I am by doing something extraordinary.“
In Yeshua I receive my identity and do not need to turn it into a trick.
Core sentence
Human beings forget the Giver when they become full; God does not forget the receiver, even in emptiness.

Short glossary

Map and land
The map is our interpretation; the land is the text itself. The map helps, but must not invent the land.
Peshat
The ordinary, primary meaning of the text within its own sentences, history and context.
The four questions
Four tests to weigh an interpretation: authority, direction, gift/requirement and coherence.
Receiving and grasping
Receiving remains in relationship with the Giver; grasping secures the gift with one’s own hand.
Mirror image
We oppose an error so strongly that we end up in the opposite error.
Gematria
A way of reading in which Hebrew letters also function as numbers. Never a foundation without a textual indication.
Haftarah
The fixed reading from the Prophets that is read with the parashah.
Habitable
An interpretation that not only sounds right, but can be lived in grace, responsibility and community.
Label
An honest designation: how certain is something and what may it do in our interpretation?
